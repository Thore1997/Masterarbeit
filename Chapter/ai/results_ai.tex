\section{Results}
This section presents the results of the previously conducted experiments.
The primary findings are summarized in Table \ref{tab:results_bench}, which details the mean and standard deviation (SD) of the $F1-Score$, $ROC AUC$, and $AUPRC$ for each evaluated method. 
To ensure a rigorous assessment of these findings, a Kruskal-Wallis test is applied for global comparison, followed by a pairwise Mann-Whitney U test.
The results of the 500 runs for each method is supplied in the appendix.
Following this evaluation, the confusion matrices for each method are displayed. 
The analysis is further extended through the plotting of $AUPRC$ and $ROC AUC$ curves to facilitate a more detailed performance comparison across all methods.

The performance utilizes F1-Score, ROC AUC, and AUPRC, accompanied by their respective standard deviations (SD). 
In the F1-Score analysis, InterCont yields the highest mean at 78.13, followed by k-NN at 68.12 and MCD at 64.14. 
The SD values indicate that k-NN maintains the lowest variability at 8.62, while MCD and InterCont show higher fluctuations at 9.41 and 10.50.


The ROC AUC results show high discriminative performance across all methods, with InterCont reaching 96.82, k-NN at 95.87, and MCD at 95.94. 
The stability of these scores is evidenced by low SD values, with k-NN demonstrating the least variance at 1.89, compared to 2.08 for InterCont and 3.59 for MCD.


For the AUPRC metric, InterCont achieves a mean of 78.23, significantly higher than MCD at 77.82 and k-NN at 72.85. 
The InterCont method exhibits near-zero variance with an SD of 14.14. 
Conversely, MCD shows the highest instability in this metric with an SD of 16.43, while k-NN records an SD of 11.54.

The Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) is a parametric test often implemented in outlier detection research to determine if the differences between the means are statistically different.
To apply this test, the data has to met two requirements: it must be normally distributed and have homogeneity of variances.
The boxplots in Figure \ref{fig:result_boxplot} indicate that the data does not meet the normality assumption.
Nevertheless, with 500 runs for each method, the central limit theorem applies, and the sampling distribution of the mean is approximately normal.

To test for homogeneity of variances, Levene's test is applied, which assesses the equality of variances across groups.
The Null of the Levene's test is as follow: 

\begin{equation}
    $H_0 = \sigma_1^2 = \sigma_2^2 = \sigma_3^2 $
\end{equation}

If the variances of the groups are not signicantly different, the Null is rejected and heterscedasticty is supposed.
