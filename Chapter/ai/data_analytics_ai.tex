To extend the analysis of the dataset's geometric structure, Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) is employed to reduce the data to a lower-dimensional space. 
UMAP has become a prominent technique in scientific literature for visualizing manifold structures, allowing for the identification of clusters or data points residing on complex, curved surfaces. 
As a non-linear dimensionality reduction method, UMAP is specifically engineered to preserve the local topological structure of the data, which is essential for interpreting high-dimensional relationships in a two-dimensional or three-dimensional projection.

\todo{why is this a representation learning algo?}
The algorithm functions by constructing a weighted $k$-nearest neighbor graph, where the parameter $k$ determines the extent of the local neighborhood considered. 
This step allows the algorithm to distinguish between regions of high density and more sparse areas of the feature space. 
By defining a local radius around these nearest neighbors, UMAP approximates the underlying manifold and subsequently optimizes a low-dimensional representation that maintains these local connectivity patterns.


The strength of the connection between a given instance and its neighbors is determined by their relative proximity.
Closer neighbors result in stronger edges, while increased distance weakens the connection. 
If instances fall outside of their respective local radii, the algorithm treats them as separated within the topological space. 
These local neighborhood representations are then combined into a global structure using a fuzzy simplicial complex. \todo{what?} 
To visualize this in a lower-dimensional space, a scatter plot is initialized with the same number of instances positioned randomly. 
Through an optimization process, instances with strong connections in the high-dimensional graph are pulled together, while those with weak or non-existent connections are pushed apart. 
This iterative refinement continues until the system reaches a low-energy state \todo{what?}, effectively capturing the global and local manifold structure of the dataset.


The configuration of UMAP requires the specification of two primary hyperparameters, which fundamentally govern the construction of the low-dimensional embedding. 
Since these parameters dictate the balance between the preservation of local and global structures, there is no universally optimal setting.
Rather, the choice depends on the specific analytical goals of the visualization.


The first hyperparameter, $n_neighbors$, determines the size of the local neighborhood UMAP considers when approximating the manifold structure. 
This parameter governs a trade-off between the preservation of local and global features in the resulting visualization. 
A low value for $n_neighbors$ constrains the algorithm to focus on very local patterns and fine-grained structures, which can be useful for identifying small clusters or intricate manifold details. 
In contrast, a high value forces the algorithm to incorporate a larger number of neighboring points, thereby prioritizing the global structure and the broader relationships between distant regions of the data space. 
Selecting an appropriate value is essential for accurately reflecting whether the data exists as a single continuous manifold or a series of discrete clusters.
The second hyperparameter, $min\_dist$, dictates the minimum allowed distance between points in the low-dimensional embedding. 
This parameter effectively controls the density of the visualization by determining how tightly points are packed together. 
A smaller minimum distance allows the algorithm to represent dense clusters more compactly, which is useful for identifying distinct groupings within the manifold. 
Conversely, a larger minimum distance forces points to maintain a greater separation, resulting in a more dispersed visualization that prioritizes the broad topological layout over local density.


To generate the visualization, the default hyperparameters were utilized, with the $n_neighbors = 15$ and $min\_dist = 0.1$. 
The primary objective of this projection was to determine whether the Wine dataset exhibits clearly defined, distinct clusters or if the classes are topologically integrated and blended together. 
By maintaining these standard settings, the algorithm provides a balanced representation that preserves both the local connectivity necessary to identify groupings and the global manifold structure required to observe the transitions between different regions of the feature space. 
This configuration allows for an objective assessment of the separability of the anomaly class from the inlier distribution in a reduced two-dimensional environment.
It is important to note that UMAP utilizes a stochastic optimization process, which implies that the specific coordinates of the resulting visualization may vary across different runs. 
However, through iterative adjustments of the hyperparameters, it was observed that the underlying cluster formations remain largely consistent.

To ensure that all attributes contribute proportionally to the manifold construction and to prevent features with larger absolute scales from dominating the projection, the data is normalized using the Z-score transformation. \todo{why this norm and not robust dist?} 
This method scales each feature by subtracting the mean and dividing by the standard deviation. 
While the mean and standard deviation can be influenced by extreme values, Z-score normalization is generally considered more robust in preserving the relative distribution of a dataset compared to Min-Max scaling, which is highly sensitive to the exact boundaries of global outliers.

Within the inlier class, the proximity of the observations suggests a lack of further distinct sub-clusters, indicating a homogeneous manifold for the normal data. 
Conversely, the clear spatial separation and high density of the outlier cluster indicate that these instances are highly distinct from the majority of the data. 
This geometric isolation suggests that the chemical profiles of the anomalies are significantly and consistently different from those of the inliers, which typically facilitates more effective detection by manifold-based and distance-based algorithms.

 
In contrast to alternative dimensionality reduction techniques, UMAP is specifically designed to preserve a greater degree of global structure. 
Nevertheless, any quantitative measurement of the exact distances between clusters should be interpreted with caution, as the transformation into a lower-dimensional space does not maintain absolute linear distances from the original manifold.
