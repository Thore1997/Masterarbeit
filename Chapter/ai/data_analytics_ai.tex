\subsubsection*{Manifold Structure Analysis}
Analysing the corrolational structure of the dataset provides bivariate insights into the dataset, but it does not capture the potential non-linear interactions or the overall geometric structure of the data.
By performing both spearman and pearson correlation, I assume that the relationships between feature are predominantly linear, hence the data lays on a flat manifold structure. 
To confirm this assumption and to gain a deeper understanding of the dataset's structure, UMAP as a non-linear dimensionality reduction technique is applied to visualize the data in a lower-dimensional space.
I will apply the Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP), eventhough the relationships seem to be linear, because UMAP can display lineare relationships as well as non-linear.
I will concentrate on the mechanics of UMAP, that influences the visualization of outliers the most and will not go into the mathematical details of the algorithm, which can be found in the original paper by \citet{mcinnes2018umap}.
Thereafter I will explain how the hyperparameters influence the visualization and how I choose the hyperparameters for my analysis.
After that I will display the resulting visualization and interpret the results.


The geometric structure, also called manifold, in the $p$-dimensional space can be highly complex globally, but locally follow a simple euklidean structure.
The goal of UMAP is to reduce the dimensional space and to display a visualization in 2D-space, while precerving the global and local geoetric structure.\citep{mcinnes2018umap}


UMAP needs the assumption that the data is uniformly distributed on the manifold structure, which allows it to estimate the local connectivity of the data points. 
This step allows the algorithm to distinguish between regions of high density and more sparse areas of the feature space. 


UMAP constructs the low dimensional depiction in two steps:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item   Construction of the high-dimensional graph by determining the local connectivity of each instance: 
    \begin{itemize}
        \item   Estimation of distances between $x_i$ and its $k$ nearest neighbors.
        \item   Calculation of $\rho$ as the distance to the nearest neighbor. 
                By subtracting $\rho$ from all neighbor distances, UMAP ensures that every instance maintains at least one strong connection, preserving the topological connectivity.
        \item   Adaptation to local density via $\sigma_i$, see equation \ref{eq:sigma}.
                Resulting in a smaller $\sigma_i$ in the locally dense regions, while a larger $\sigma_i$ in sparse regions.
        \item   Construction of a fuzzy simplicial set to merge these local views into a consistent global topological structure.
    \end{itemize}
    \item   Generating the low-dimensional representation:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item   Instead of random placement, UMAP uses spectral embedding to provide a stable initial layout. 
                This captures the global topology early on and prevents the optimization from converging into poor local minima.
        \item   The algorithm iteratively adjusts the 2D positions by minimizing the fuzzy set cross-entropy.
        \item   Using Stochastic Gradient Descent, points with high connectivity are pulled together (attraction), while unconnected points are pushed apart (repulsion). 
                This continues until convergence is achieved.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

The equation \ref{eq:sigma} describes how UMAP adapts the local connectivity and determines the parameter $\sigma_i$.

\begin{equation}
    \sum_{j=1}^{k} \exp \left( \frac{-\max(0, d(x_i, x_j) - \rho_i)}{\sigma_i} \right) = \log_2(k)
     \label{eq:sigma}
\end{equation}

Where$d(x_i, x_j) - \rho_i$ represents the weight of the connection between instance $x_i$ and its neighbor $x_j$.
By selecting the nearest neighbor and subtracting the same distance UMAP assigns the strongest connection to the nearest neighbor, hence $\exp(0) = 1$.
By applying the exponential function, UMAP ensures that the influence of neighbors decreases with distance, creating a smooth decay in connectivity.
$\sigma_i$ has to be set that the sum of the exponential function over all neighbors equals $\log_2(k)$.
$\log_2(k)$ was chosen, because it ensures that the entropy of all neighbors are consistent.
A nummerical optimzation process, known as binary search is applyied to find the appropriate $\sigma_i$ for each instance.


To meet this requirement, UMAP assigns in locally dense regions a smaller $\sigma_i$, which effectively stretches the space.
Conversely, in sparse regions, a larger $\sigma_i$ compresses the space.
This ensures the assumption of uniform distribution is met.


On the one hand, that depicts an outlier as an isolated instance apprart from the main cluster, which coincides with the global outlier definition. 
On the other hand, the outliers is still binded to the manifold through the nearest neighbor.
Therefore the absolut distance in the diagram looses its meaning. 


When UMAP generates the low-dimensional representation, it uses attraction and repulsion to position the points in the 2D space.
Attraction occurs between points that are strongly connected in high-dimensional space.
If data points do not have a connection to another instance at all, repulsion forces them apart in the low-dimensional space.
Those forces are adaptive to the weights $d(x_i, x_{i_j}) - \rho_i$
This results in a layout where loose clusters of are grouped together, even if they are sparsely placed in the high-dimensional space.


\subsubsection*{Hyperparameters}
The configuration of UMAP requires the specification of two primary hyperparameters, which fundamentally govern the construction of the low-dimensional embedding. 
Since these parameters dictate the balance between the preservation of local and global structures, there is no universally optimal setting.
Rather, the choice depends on the specific analytical goals of the visualization.


The first hyperparameter, $n_ {neighbors}$, determines the size of the local neighborhood UMAP considers when approximating the manifold structure. 
This parameter governs a trade-off between the preservation of local and global features in the resulting visualization. 
A low value for $n_ {neighbors}$ constrains the algorithm to focus on very local patterns and fine-grained structures, which can be useful for identifying small clusters or intricate manifold details. 
In contrast, a high value forces the algorithm to incorporate a larger number of neighboring points, thereby prioritizing the global structure and the broader relationships between distant regions of the data space. 
Selecting an appropriate value is essential for accurately reflecting whether the data exists as a single continuous manifold or a series of discrete clusters.


The second hyperparameter, $min_{dist}$, dictates the minimum allowed distance between points in the low-dimensional embedding. 
This parameter effectively controls the density of the visualization by determining how tightly points are packed together. 
A smaller minimum distance allows the algorithm to represent dense clusters more compactly, which is useful for identifying distinct groupings within the manifold. 
Conversely, a larger minimum distance forces points to maintain a greater separation, resulting in a more dispersed visualization that prioritizes the broad topological layout over local density.


To generate the visualization, the default hyperparameters were utilized, with the $n_{neighbors} = 15$ and $min_{dist} = 0.1$. 
The primary objective of this projection was to determine whether the Wine dataset exhibits clearly defined, distinct clusters or if the classes are topologically integrated and blended together. 
By maintaining these standard settings, the algorithm provides a balanced representation that preserves both the local connectivity necessary to identify groupings and the global manifold structure required to observe the transitions between different regions of the feature space. 
This configuration allows for an objective assessment of the separability of the anomaly class from the inlier distribution in a reduced two-dimensional environment.b
It is important to note that UMAP utilizes a stochastic optimization process, which implies that the specific coordinates of the resulting visualization may vary across different runs. 
However, through iterative adjustments of the hyperparameters, it was observed that the underlying cluster formations remain largely consistent.
